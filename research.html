Job Market Paper

Rebalancing Supply Networks after a Geopolitical Shock

This paper studies how import sourcing networks adjust to geopolitical supply shocks. Using the 2019 Korea–Japan trade dispute, I show that imports from Japan declined more for products with concentrated alternative sourcing, while imports from third countries increased relatively more. The adjustment occurred primarily through rebalancing within pre-existing supplier relationships rather than through an expansion in the number of supplier countries. Within these relationships, imports also shifted relatively away from countries with greater pre-dispute geopolitical risk.

Working Paper

Trade Imbalance and Reciprocity in the WTO: Rule-Based Adjustment Framework

with Mostafa Beshkar

This paper examines how persistent trade imbalances generate deviations in trade concessions from their original WTO benchmark. We develop a multi-country structural trade model and quantify the tariff adjustments required to restore reciprocity, documenting a strong relationship between bilateral trade imbalances and reciprocity-restoring tariff changes. Motivated by these findings, we propose a rule-based framework inspired by Keynes's principle of symmetric adjustment that assigns adjustment responsibilities to both deficit and surplus countries.

Work in Progress

Economic Concessions and Political Alignment

This project studies the relationship between economic concessions in trade agreements and political alignment across countries. It examines whether countries receiving greater economic concessions exhibit greater political alignment with the United States, using measures of bilateral defense cooperation and UN General Assembly voting similarity to characterize countries' political relationships with the United States.
