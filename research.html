Latent Statecraft: Political Alignment in the Shadow of Tariffs

with Mostafa Beshkar

The terms-of-trade concessions exchanged under the GATT/WTO are far from reciprocal: the United States concedes substantially more than it receives from many of its partners. We examine whether the United States was compensated for these net concessions in political rather than economic terms, a form of leverage we call latent statecraft. Measuring concessions as the terms-of-trade value of bilateral tariff cuts, which is revalued as trade patterns shift even when tariffs do not change, we find that among transactional partners, larger concessions are associated with closer alignment with the United States in UN General Assembly voting. No such relationship appears among security allies, and the response is concentrated in votes the United States does not designate as important.

Trade Imbalance and Reciprocity in the WTO: Rule-Based Adjustment Framework

with Mostafa Beshkar

This paper examines how persistent trade imbalances generate deviations in trade concessions from their original WTO benchmark. We develop a multi-country structural trade model and quantify the tariff adjustments required to restore reciprocity, documenting a strong relationship between bilateral trade imbalances and reciprocity-restoring tariff changes. Motivated by these findings, we propose a rule-based framework inspired by Keynes's principle of symmetric adjustment that assigns adjustment responsibilities to both deficit and surplus countries.

Rebalancing Sourcing Networks after a Geopolitical Shock (Draft available upon request)

This paper studies how import sourcing networks adjust to geopolitical supply shocks. Using the 2019 Korea–Japan trade dispute, I show that products with more concentrated alternative sourcing adjust primarily through reallocation across incumbent supplier countries rather than through greater expansion of the supplier base or aggregate third-country imports. Within the existing sourcing network, reallocation is also tilted away from suppliers with greater pre-dispute geopolitical risk.
